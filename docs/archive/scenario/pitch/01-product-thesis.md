# Product Thesis

## One-Line Pitch

Dream of One is a Korean-first 3D social-stealth game where NPC interactions are driven by Codex CLI workers that generate social pressure, while deterministic Station rules convert imprecise speech into Evidence, Inquest, and verdict.

## Player Promise

The player is not solving a case. The player is becoming one.

Moment to moment, the player must:

- read public rules before entering social pressure;
- perform ordinary procedure under observation;
- choose bounded speech acts;
- watch NPCs turn mistakes into records;
- return to the Station and survive formal comparison.

## Why It Is Fundable

| Requirement | Plan |
|---|---|
| Clear hook | "Codex-driven NPC society investigates you, but the verdict is deterministic." |
| Contained scope | One compact civic loop: Station, Store, Studio, Park, Station. |
| Strong vertical slice | 10-30 minutes proving text danger, AI pressure, Evidence, and verdict. |
| Distinct genre position | Social stealth + procedural bureaucracy + Codex CLI-driven NPC interaction. |
| Low asset risk | Free/CC0 stylized 3D assets with authored lighting, text, and UI priority. |
| Technical defensibility | Codex CLI output is proposal-only; backend owns state and validation. |

## Target Audience

Primary:

- players of narrative indies, social deduction-adjacent games, and procedural pressure games;
- players who like `Papers, Please`, `Beholder`, `Orwell`, `The Stanley Parable`, `Return of the Obra Dinn`, and surreal institutional horror.

Secondary:

- AI-curious players who want AI reactivity but still expect game rules to be fair;
- Korean players interested in institutional horror built from Korean source language.

## Comparable Lessons

| Reference | Lesson To Use | What Not To Copy |
|---|---|---|
| `Papers, Please` | Procedure creates pressure when rules are legible and escalating. | Passport stamping and border-control skin. |
| `Hitman` | Social stealth needs readable public/private behavior rules. | Large sandbox assassination scope. |
| `The Stanley Parable` | Authored response to player disobedience can make simple spaces feel alive. | Comic meta narrator. |
| `Obra Dinn` | UI and evidence structures can be the drama surface. | Ship/death reconstruction premise. |
| `Control` | Institutional text can make the uncanny feel official. | Action combat and paranormal spectacle. |

## Product Pillars

| Pillar | Pitch Meaning | Shipping Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Codex pressure, rule-owned verdict | Codex makes social surface reactive; backend makes outcomes fair. | Every Codex-generated line maps to validated action, artifact, or no-op. |
| Text is danger | Signs, prompts, barks, reports, and why-lines are gameplay objects. | Every player-facing text has trigger, state, and Evidence role. |
| Korean institutional horror | The source voice is public notice, intake desk, and case log. | Korean is authored first; English preserves function and coldness. |
| Small but authored 3D space | The world feels designed, not large. | Routes, sightlines, props, and lighting teach procedure. |
| Fail-forward social stealth | Bad answers narrow options instead of resetting. | Mistakes create records, witnesses, and Station thresholds. |

## What Must Be Proven Before Funding

- Codex CLI NPC proposal path produces varied but bounded pressure.
- Backend rejects invalid Codex proposals and still emits fallback Evidence.
- Player can understand why Exposure changed.
- Korean source text feels authored and not machine-generic.
- One polished 10-30 minute vertical slice is playable without docs.
