# Lane 02: Workplace, Life, And Routine Simulator Benchmarks

Date: 2026-05-14
Lane: workplace, life, routine, and social simulation
Project lens: Dream of One, Same Order Store -> Station

## 1. Scope And Source Policy

This lane looks at English-language web sources for games that make ordinary life, workplace procedure, social routine, or small-service labor playable. The goal is not to find genre labels for Dream of One. The goal is to identify benchmarkable simulator patterns that can support a mundane store/station routine before Dream Law, Cover Test language, Exposure, inquest, verdict, or LLM flourish.

Source priority was official pages, Steam/storefront pages, developer pages, developer interviews, and reputable games press. Steam pages are treated as official publisher storefront copy. Reputable reviews/interviews are used where the relevant lesson is about social texture, routine NPCs, or mundane labor design rather than a feature list. No long verbatim quotes are used.

Dream of One adoption rule: steal ordinary procedures, social recurrence, service correctness, and evidence-producing state. Do not steal detective framing. The player is not an investigator. NPCs and Station systems investigate the player. Text is where the danger starts. Provider or LLM output may vary wording only; deterministic backend/runtime owns suspicion signals, Evidence, reports, inquest, verdict, and session end.

## 2. Benchmark Cards

### The Sims / The Sims 4

Source URLs: [EA features](https://www.ea.com/games/the-sims/the-sims-4/features), [EA careers guide](https://help.ea.com/en/articles/the-sims/the-sims-4/the-sims-4-careers/)

- Game: The Sims 4.
- Core player job: Household director. The player creates Sims, homes, aspirations, relationships, venues, and career paths.
- Routine loop: Manage needs and mood, send Sims through work or home tasks, build social ties, improve skills, buy or build, sleep, repeat.
- NPC/social texture: Sims have personalities, aspirations, social venues, parties, neighborhoods, and relationship play. The social system is broad rather than procedural.
- Economy/time pressure: Careers have schedules, promotion requirements, daily tasks, reward items, and money pressure through household bills and purchases.
- Progression: Career ranks, skills, aspiration goals, home expansion, unlocked objects, and relationship or family outcomes.
- What Dream of One can steal: Small visible obligations make life readable. A daily schedule, a work panel, and "complete this ordinary requirement" can support pressure without lore. Dream can compress this into a Store procedure panel: queue position, order label, receipt state, report risk.
- What to avoid: Godlike control, broad life management, and too many currencies. Dream should not become a dollhouse or an all-purpose life sim. The player must feel like a person being socially read, not an omniscient manager.

### Stardew Valley

Source URLs: [official about page](https://www.stardewvalley.net/about/)

- Game: Stardew Valley.
- Core player job: New farmer and community member rebuilding a life in Pelican Town.
- Routine loop: Wake up, choose a day plan around energy, weather, season, money, and town schedules, then farm, forage, mine, fish, gift, shop, return home, sleep.
- NPC/social texture: Over 30 residents have daily schedules, birthdays, weekly/yearly dialogue variation, cutscenes, festivals, personal requests, friendship, romance, and family outcomes.
- Economy/time pressure: Day clock, energy, crop seasons, tool upgrades, money, bundles, and the Joja/community center choice keep routine play goal-directed.
- Progression: Five skill areas, recipes, professions, new areas, farm growth, community restoration, relationships, and marriage.
- What Dream of One can steal: Calendar and schedule are content multipliers. NPC recurrence matters more than NPC volume. A small set of store/station characters can feel alive if they change location, lines, and pressure by day and by prior record.
- What to avoid: Farm sprawl, combat breadth, collection completionism, and cozy reward inflation. Dream needs routine as public self-maintenance, not a productivity garden.

### Animal Crossing: New Horizons

Source URLs: [Nintendo official site](https://animalcrossing.nintendo.com/new-horizons/)

- Game: Animal Crossing: New Horizons.
- Core player job: Island resident, caretaker, collector, decorator, and community builder.
- Routine loop: Explore the island, collect bugs or fish, gather resources, craft tools and furniture, decorate, visit residents, and return across real-time days.
- NPC/social texture: Residents, birthdays, visitors, island events, and casual greetings make return visits feel socially anchored even when pressure is low.
- Economy/time pressure: Bells, Nook Miles, debt, resource availability, shop cycles, and real-time rhythms create gentle pacing.
- Progression: Home expansion, museum and collection growth, island customization, resident additions, seasonal content, and personal decoration.
- What Dream of One can steal: A familiar place can carry routine pressure if the player returns to it often. Store signage, a receipt tray, a known queue mark, and a repeated clerk can become legible in the same way an Animal Crossing island becomes legible.
- What to avoid: Decoration-first motivation and frictionless safety. Dream should not make ordinary society feel like a personal paradise. It should feel helpful until the player's words fail the local script.

### Shenmue Routine NPCs

Source URLs: [PC Gamer on Shenmue NPCs](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-secret-lives-of-shenmues-npcs/)

- Game: Shenmue / Shenmue I & II.
- Core player job: Ryo investigates his father's murder, but the benchmark value for Dream is the town routine, not the investigation.
- Routine loop: Move through a small town by time of day, wait for shops or appointments, ask residents, work jobs, train, use arcades or vending machines, sleep, repeat.
- NPC/social texture: NPCs are memorable because they appear in repeated places and routines, with implied lives beyond the player's objective.
- Economy/time pressure: Clock time, shop hours, appointments, buses, part-time work, and money give the world a daily cadence.
- Progression: Story gates, new information, jobs, fights, and route changes move the player through the town.
- What Dream of One can steal: Independent routine makes society feel like it exists without the player. Dream only needs a few NPCs with visible schedules and pressure functions: Clerk opens register, queue witness waits, manager reviews reports, Station officer receives records.
- What to avoid: Detective clue loops, martial revenge structure, and a high handcrafted biography burden. Dream should not ask the player to interrogate society. Society should inspect the player's public self.

### Lake

Source URLs: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1118240/Lake/?l=english), [OPB article/interview](https://www.opb.org/article/2022/05/02/gamious-narrative-games-playstation-lake-oregon-town/)

- Game: Lake.
- Core player job: Substitute mail carrier in Providence Oaks for two weeks.
- Routine loop: Receive a daily address list, choose a delivery route, drive, deliver letters and packages, talk to residents, choose after-work activity, repeat.
- NPC/social texture: The same postal route exposes Meredith to familiar faces, new residents, old relationships, possible romance, and a town that becomes spatially familiar.
- Economy/time pressure: Pressure is deliberately low. The main structure is duty, route completion, and the two-week return-or-stay decision.
- Progression: Town familiarity, relationship choices, optional errands, and final life-direction endings.
- What Dream of One can steal: A route sheet is a powerful low-cost simulator contract. Dream can use a Store procedure sheet and Station intake sheet: where to stand, what to say, what receipt exists, what report moves next.
- What to avoid: Consequence softness. Lake can let the postal routine stay gentle; Dream needs each public mismatch to become a possible record.

### Coffee Talk

Source URLs: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/914800/Coffee_Talk/), [Toge Productions itch page](https://togeproductions.itch.io/coffee-talk), [Toge devlog on brewing mechanics](https://togeproductions.itch.io/coffee-talk-demo/devlog/91110/coffee-talk-devlog-4-brewing-the-gameplay-mechanics-and-latte-artfeature)

- Game: Coffee Talk.
- Core player job: Night-shift barista and listener.
- Routine loop: Customer enters, talks through a problem, the player brews a drink from stocked ingredients, serves it, and the conversation or later branch reflects service.
- NPC/social texture: Recurring fantasy-modern customers bring work, romance, identity, and family problems into a familiar cafe frame.
- Economy/time pressure: Low direct economy pressure. The meaningful check is whether the player understands the order, ingredients, and customer need.
- Progression: Recipes, customer storylines, repeat visits, and branching outcomes determined by service rather than dialogue menu choice.
- What Dream of One can steal: Service can be dialogue. Preparing the correct drink is a nonverbal answer to the customer. Dream can make "confirm same order," "correct receipt," and "record statement" the actual choice, with text as risk.
- What to avoid: Passive listening and opaque recipe correctness. Dream's player must see how a line maps to a procedure, signal, repair, or report.

### VA-11 Hall-A

Source URLs: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/447530/VA11_HallA_Cyberpunk_Bartender_Action/), [PlayStation Blog port post](https://blog.playstation.com/2019/04/08/va-11-hall-a-serves-up-drinks-on-ps4-may-2/)

- Game: VA-11 Hall-A: Cyberpunk Bartender Action.
- Core player job: Bartender at VA-11 Hall-A in Glitch City.
- Routine loop: Read client mood and preference, mix a drink, serve, listen, learn about daily life, and continue through bar shifts.
- NPC/social texture: The bar gathers recurring clients whose tastes, preoccupations, and stories create a dense social world from a single workplace.
- Economy/time pressure: The key pressure is not speed. It is remembering recipes, client taste, and the fact that drink service changes story direction.
- Progression: Client arcs, branching story through prepared drinks, and accumulating knowledge of people and place.
- What Dream of One can steal: A client's taste is a pressure function. Store Clerk can have a procedural taste: queue order, label consistency, receipt cleanliness. Station Officer can have one: answer format, contradiction control.
- What to avoid: Text charm as the whole game. Dream cannot rely on excellent conversations alone. Runtime state must be inspectable, replayable, and deterministic.

### Strange Horticulture

Source URLs: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1574580/Strange_Horticulture/), [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/finding-right-plant-job-strange-horticulture), [PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/strange-horticulture-review/)

- Game: Strange Horticulture.
- Core player job: Proprietor of a local plant store and identifier of strange plants.
- Routine loop: A customer requests a plant, often with partial information; the player inspects plants, cross-references an encyclopedia, labels or selects the plant, serves the customer, then gains clues, plants, or story movement.
- NPC/social texture: Customers, notes, map clues, coven/cult choices, and occult town rumors pass through the shop counter.
- Economy/time pressure: Day structure, wrong-plant risk, growing inventory, and increasingly specific clue reading create cognitive pressure more than money pressure.
- Progression: More plant entries, more tools, map discoveries, alchemy, branching outcomes, and deeper town mystery.
- What Dream of One can steal: The shop counter can be the whole world if the tools are tactile and the records are clear. Receipts, labels, order boards, report slips, and Station dossiers can replace plants and encyclopedia pages.
- What to avoid: Turning Dream into an occult deduction game. The player should not solve a mystery by identifying hidden objects. They should maintain cover while others classify their text.

### Good Pizza, Great Pizza

Source URLs: [Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/770810/_/?l=english)

- Game: Good Pizza, Great Pizza.
- Core player job: Pizzeria owner/cook trying to keep the shop running.
- Routine loop: Read a customer order, interpret the phrasing, assemble pizza, bake, cut, serve, earn money, improve equipment or decor, and advance chapter stories.
- NPC/social texture: Customer requests, rival pizzeria pressure, Pizza News Network, chapter characters, and recurring comic situations give personality to a narrow service counter.
- Economy/time pressure: Ingredient use, order accuracy, customer throughput, equipment upgrades, and shop survival make language and execution matter.
- Progression: New toppings, equipment upgrades, decor, chapters, seasonal events, and shop features.
- What Dream of One can steal: Ambiguous natural-language service orders can be a deterministic game. "Same order" should work like a pizza order: the wording is human, but the fulfillment check is exact and explainable.
- What to avoid: Dexterity pressure and unclear customer phrasing as comedy. Dream's failures must name the mismatch; otherwise the player cannot learn the social script.

### Cart Life

Source URLs: [PC Gamer on open source release](https://www.pcgamer.com/cart-life-open-source/), [Destructoid review](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-cart-life/), [Video Games for the Arts note](https://www.videogamesforthearts.com/articles/cartlife)

- Game: Cart Life.
- Core player job: Street vendor or small retail worker under personal, legal, and economic pressure.
- Routine loop: Buy supplies, prepare inventory, commute, open a cart, serve small transactions, handle hunger, sleep, appointments, permits, and other obligations.
- NPC/social texture: Ordinary customer contact, bureaucracy, domestic obligations, and small humiliations create social weight without grand plot.
- Economy/time pressure: Thin margins, tiny sales, food, rent, permits, appointments, and court dates turn routine into survival pressure.
- Progression: Survival across days, character-specific obligations, and the emotional cost of work rather than conventional power growth.
- What Dream of One can steal: Mundane bureaucracy can be dramatic. A receipt annotation, queue mismatch, manager note, or intake form can feel heavier than a combat encounter if it threatens future standing.
- What to avoid: Friction as misery. Dream should create procedural dread, not punish players with exhausting chores or opaque interface hardship.

## 3. Cross-Game Pattern Extraction

- Job verbs before lore. The best benchmarks make the player understand the work first: manage household, farm, deliver mail, brew coffee, mix drinks, identify plants, make pizza, sell goods. Dream's baseline verb is not "discover truth." It is "answer ordinary procedure while preserving a public self."
- Day or shift contracts make routine legible. A daily address list, work shift, farm day, store opening, or customer queue tells the player what normal means before anything goes wrong.
- Recurrence beats scale. A small number of returning NPCs with schedules, preoccupations, and visible duties can feel richer than a large cast of one-off dialogue.
- Service correctness can carry narrative consequence. Coffee Talk, VA-11 Hall-A, Strange Horticulture, and Good Pizza, Great Pizza all make the work act as the story input. Dream should make receipt confirmation, label language, and recorded statements carry consequence.
- Ambiguity needs a stable judge. Natural language can be expressive only if the game owns the classifier. Dream must let wording be dangerous while keeping suspicion, Evidence, and repair deterministic.
- Progression can be familiarity. The player learning the store layout, queue marks, receipt grammar, and Station format may be more valuable than adding new systems.
- Economy and time pressure should be narrow. The useful pressure is "I have to answer now, and the answer is recorded," not a broad money optimizer.
- Social texture comes from preoccupations. Store Clerk cares about queue records. Manager cares about exceptions. Station Officer cares about answer format. These are more actionable than biographies.
- Cut breadth until the mundane loop reads. Dream should not add Dream Law spectacle or LLM flourish until players can explain the normal store procedure and why one answer damaged their record.

## 4. Dream Of One Adoption Plan

The mundane baseline should exist before dream law, LLM flourish, or broad location expansion. The first simulator proof should be a small ordinary society with a record pipeline.

Minimum Store baseline:

- Space: entrance, queue mark, counter, label board, payment point, receipt printer or tray, report tray, exit.
- Player verbs: enter, stand in queue, inspect label board, answer clerk, confirm same order, correct receipt, ask for repair, pay, take receipt, leave.
- NPCs: Store Clerk, Queue Witness, Manager as offscreen or later handoff, Station Officer.
- Procedure fields: expected_item, expected_label, item_count, queue_position, receipt_id, statement_id, local_fit, mismatch_code, repair_allowed, report_weight, exposure_delta, station_transition.
- Visible artifacts: receipt, correction slip, queue note, clerk statement, manager exception record, Station intake dossier.
- Dialogue lanes: safe/local, repair, risky/weird, recorded statement. All four map to deterministic signal outcomes before provider wording is involved.

Minimum daily loop:

1. Day start: player enters Same Order Store and sees the normal public procedure.
2. Store exchange: clerk asks routine confirmation; player answers through choices or recorded statement.
3. Local read: clerk reaction shows whether the answer fit the local script.
4. Artifact: receipt or report slip is produced with a why-line.
5. Propagation: soft concern stays local; hard mismatch moves to manager/Station.
6. Station intake: officer compares statement, receipt, and report in fact order.
7. Outcome: repair, warning, inquest, verdict, or session end is deterministic.

Prototype route set:

- Clean route: same order confirmed, receipt consistent, no report.
- Repair route: player admits uncertainty in local terms, receives small cost and corrected record.
- Soft report route: public mismatch creates clerk concern and manager note, but no immediate end.
- Hard inquest route: recorded statement contradicts store procedure, enters Station intake, and closes deterministically.

LLM/provider adoption rule:

- Add provider wording only after deterministic fallback text proves the loop.
- Provider may vary clerk courtesy, queue murmurs, Station phrasing, and localized line variants.
- Provider must not decide fit, suspicion signal, Evidence validity, report weight, Exposure delta, verdict, or session end.

## 5. Narrative/Content Proof Gates And Cut Rules

Proof gates:

- Routine legibility gate: A fresh player can say where to stand, what "same order" means, what the receipt records, and what went wrong after a mismatch.
- Player-role gate: The player is never asked to investigate hidden truth. NPCs and Station systems inspect the player's speech, consistency, and procedure fit.
- Consequence gate: Every risky line has a deterministic mismatch code, why-line, report or exposure delta, and repair status.
- Content-budget gate: Every NPC card names public role, protected procedure, noticed mismatch, assumption about the player, soft pressure move, hard pressure move, report artifact, repair, and provider boundary.
- Branch-collapse gate: Safe, repair, risky/weird, and recorded-statement lanes collapse into inspectable state, not endless bespoke dialogue.
- Visible-text gate: The same canonical answer in Korean and English produces the same consequence. Wording variants cannot change authority.
- Playable proof gate: A route is not accepted by prose description alone. It needs Godot-visible behavior, backend validation, Evidence JSON, and a screenshot or capture that shows the player-facing consequence.

Cut rules:

- Cut any lore beat that explains the dream but does not change what the player does, reads, risks, or records.
- Cut any NPC whose pressure function is only biography.
- Cut any location that lacks a protected public procedure.
- Cut any branch that cannot produce a canonical signal, repair state, or report artifact.
- Cut broad schedules beyond Store and Station until the Same Order loop is readable.
- Cut LLM flourish until deterministic fallback wording proves the same route.
- Cut hidden deduction. Dream is not about finding clues. It is about being read by a society that treats ordinary procedure as authority.
- Cut economy expansion if it turns the game into money optimization. Keep payment, receipt, and record pressure first.
