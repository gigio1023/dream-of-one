# Scenario Research Source Map

This source map captures the research spine used to rebuild the Dream of One scenario bible.

## Research Lanes

| Lane | Focus | Main Takeaway |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Social stealth | Start every scenario with an accusation vector. NPCs are examiners of normality, not quest dispensers. |
| 2 | Surreal bureaucratic horror | Horror comes from classification pressure: the player is converted into a case. |
| 3 | Narrative frameworks | Use 3-act/5-pressure pacing, MDA, storylets, fail-forward, and bark systems. |
| 4 | Indie case studies | Strong indie authorship comes from constraint, ritual, text, sound, and scene composition. |
| 5 | Local canon audit | Active canon exists, but scenario detail is too thin and some older org/NPC docs drift from runtime IDs. |
| 6 | Episode structure | Store, Studio, Park, and Station can form one 20-30 minute civic loop. |
| 7 | Characters and dialogue | NPCs need procedural voice cards, report triggers, barks, and why-line templates. |
| 8 | Environmental storytelling | Every landmark needs props, routes, sightlines, text placement, lighting, and audio motifs with narrative purpose. |
| 9 | Korean-first localization | Korean institutional register should be the primary tone, not a translation of English bureaucracy. |
| 10 | Quality bar | A scenario is game-ready only when beats, states, Evidence, text, implementation hooks, and playtest checks are explicit. |

## Synthesis Rules

- Do not copy passports, ghost ships, meta narrators, haunted houses, Southern Gothic, or Disco-style skill voices.
- Copy production methods: narrow loop, strong procedure, authored reactivity, constrained interface, theatrical staging, and text as system.
- Keep immediate rules legible and cosmology partially withheld.
- Make every failure produce forward motion: a record, a witness, a restriction, a rumor, or a Station threshold crossing.
- Treat Korean as the authored source language for UI and institutional voice.
